  [slide3.xml]: Triadic processes Link between micro-processes and macro-signatures of complex networks Micro-process: "friends of friends become friends." Macro-signature: Number of fully connected triples deviates significantly from that characteristic of random networks This has implications for: Diffusion dynamics within networks Overall cohesion within the social structure (e.g. community structure) Brokerage and information control opportunities Innovation and the generation of novel ideas

  [slide37.xml]: Generalized balance  Jammed states: "...represent situations in which divided loyalties obscure group boundaries" (van de Rijt 2011: 97-98) Suggest that the pursuit of structural balance at the micro-level does not necessarily result in generalized balance at the macro-level Having to satisfy multiple constraints prevents binary polarization

  [slide20.xml]: The social network analysis three-step shuffle  Think up or grab an “off the shelf” fuzzy concept from social science to develop a  network-theoretic concept (e.g. popularity, brokerage, reciprocity, role, status) Translate the concept from natural language to the “language” of graph theory to develop a  graph-theoretic concept ( e.g. points and lines) Link the graph-theoretic concept to matrix algebra to develop a  network-analytic measure  of the network-theoretic concept  (e.g. indegree centrality as a measure of popularity)

  [slide25.xml]: Graph theory: Representing network concepts “Simple” (binary, undirected) graphs can represent some social network concepts: E.g. “having a tie,” “having lots of connections,” “being a broker between two people” But not all E.g. “Reciprocity” can only be represented in a  directed  graph The representation of other social network concepts require other types of graphs: For instance, “having ties of different strengths” can only be represented by a graph with non-binary values

  [slide7.xml]: Social Foci Key insight:  People do not form ties at random, but rather they form ties through settings where they meet and interact with others Social foci   “ a social, psychological, legal, or physical entity around which joint activities are organized (e.g., workplaces, voluntary organizations, hangouts, families, etc.” (Feld 1981: 1016) Tie formation via affiliations (e.g., clubs, religious organizations, classes) Foci vary in constraint (time and energy required) and size People can belong to multiple intersecting foci Can be cross-cutting or overlapping Composition of foci → composition of ego networks Segregated foci → homophily Integrated foci → reduce homophily
