# Plan: Creating a Standalone Directed Graphs Chapter

This plan outlines the creation of a standalone chapter, `lesson-graphs-directed.qmd`, that consolidates all scattered directed graph concepts (neighborhoods, degrees, average degree, and density) into a single, cohesive lesson. This new chapter will be placed directly after the basic introduction to graphs in the book's structure.

---

## 1. Structure of the New Chapter (`lesson-graphs-directed.qmd`)

The new chapter will contain the following integrated sections:

1. **Introduction to Directed Graphs (Digraphs)**:
   - Formally introduce directed graphs, where edges are ordered pairs of vertices called **arcs**.
   - Point-and-line diagram of a directed graph (generating `fig-directed` here) to show incoming and outgoing arrows.
   - Conceptual background on directed relationships in social networks (social support, texting, nominations).
2. **Node Neighborhoods in Directed Graphs**:
   - Define **in-neighbors** ($N^{in}(v)$) and **out-neighbors** ($N^{out}(v)$).
   - Show how reciprocal/mutual ties occur when a node belongs to both sets.
3. **Node Degree in Directed Graphs**:
   - Define **indegree** ($k^{in}$) and **outdegree** ($k^{out}$).
   - Classify node behavioral roles based on degree properties (from `@Harary65`):
     - **Receivers**: $k^{in} > 0$ and $k^{out} = 0$.
     - **Carriers**: $k^{in} > 0$ and $k^{out} > 0$.
     - **Transmitters**: $k^{in} = 0$ and $k^{out} > 0$.
4. **Directed Graph Metrics**:
   - **Average Degree in Directed Graphs**: Explain why there is no "doubling" needed, so average degree is simply $\bar{k} = \frac{m}{n}$.
   - **Directed Graph Density**: Define the maximum possible arcs as $n(n-1)$ and calculate density as $d(G)^d = \frac{m}{n(n-1)}$.
