## 1. New Slide Additions

### Slide 1.1: Hamiltonian Paths & Cycles
We will insert a new slide `## Hamiltonian Paths & Cycles` immediately after `## Graph Circumference: Longest Cycle`:
- **Hamiltonian Path**: A path that visits *every single node* in the graph exactly once.
- **Hamiltonian Cycle**: A cycle that visits *every single node* in the graph exactly once and returns to the origin.
- **Visual Example**:
  - We will render our 5-node house graph `gr_undir` on the right:
    - **Hamiltonian Path exists**: e.g., $B \rightarrow E \rightarrow C \rightarrow A \rightarrow D$ (visits $B, E, C, A, D$ exactly once, highlighted in red).
    - **No Hamiltonian Cycle exists**: Explain the intuitive proof: node $B$ is a "leaf node" of degree 1. For any cycle to visit $B$, it must enter and leave $B$ via different edges, requiring $B$ to have a degree of at least 2. Since $k_B = 1$, no Hamiltonian cycle can exist.

### Slide 1.3: The Connectivity Inequality
We will insert a new slide `## The Connectivity Inequality` right after `## Graph Cutsets: Quantitative Cohesion`:
- Formulate the fundamental connectivity theorem:
  $$
    \kappa(G) \le \lambda(G) \le \delta(G)
  $$
  where:
  - $\kappa(G)$ is the vertex (node) connectivity of the graph.
  - $\lambda(G)$ is the edge connectivity of the graph.
  - $\delta(G)$ is the minimum degree of the graph.
- **Why this holds**: Explain that removing a vertex deletes all its incident edges, so it is always easier or equal to disconnect a graph by removing nodes than by removing edges, and both are bounded by the minimum degree (as you can always isolate the least-connected node by removing its neighbors/edges).
- **Concrete Example**: Use our house graph `gr_undir`:
  - $\kappa(G) = 1$ (node $E$ is a cutpoint).
  - $\lambda(G) = 1$ (the bridge edge $B-E$).
  - $\delta(G) = 1$ (node $B$ has degree 1).
  - Here, $1 \le 1 \le 1$ holds with perfect equality!
