# Plan: Before and After Cutset Visualizations in `slides/graph-connectivity.qmd`

This plan outlines the design and implementation of side-by-side "Before and After" graph visualizations for all four edge and node cutset slides in `slides/graph-connectivity.qmd` to clearly demonstrate the structural effects of cutting edges or removing vertices.

---

## 1. Visual Design of Before-and-After Comparisons

Using `patchwork`'s `+` operator, we will display two graphs side-by-side on each of the four cutset slides:
* **The "Before" Graph (`p_before`)**: Displays the original connected graph, with the target cutset edges or vertices highlighted in **bright red**.
* **The "After" Graph (`p_after`)**: Displays the resulting disconnected graph, showing exactly how removing those targeted edges/nodes splits the graph into separate, unreachable components.

---

## 2. Step-by-Step R Chunk Implementations

### Slide 2.1: `## Edge Cutsets`
* **Before**: Highlight edges $A-C$ (1-3) and $B-C$ (2-3) in red on `gr_cutset`.
* **After**: Remove those two edges using `filter(!((from == 1 & to == 3) | (from == 2 & to == 3)))`, showing $\{C, D, E\}$ split from $\{A, B\}$.
* **Layout**: Side-by-side (`p_before + p_after`), with `fig-width: 8` and `fig-height: 4.5`.

### Slide 2.2: `## Bridges`
* **Before**: Highlight the single bridge edge $C-D$ (3-4) in red on `gr_bridge`.
* **After**: Remove the bridge edge, showing $\{A, B, C\}$ completely disconnected from $\{D, E\}$.
* **Layout**: Side-by-side (`p_before + p_after`), with `fig-width: 8` and `fig-height: 4.5`.

### Slide 2.3: `## Edge Cutset: A Visual Example`
* **Before**: Highlight edges $C-A$ (3-5) and $D-A$ (4-5) in red on `gr_undir`.
* **After**: Remove those two edges, showing node $A$ completely isolated from the rest of the house graph.
* **Layout**: Side-by-side (`p_before + p_after`), with `fig-width: 8` and `fig-height: 4.5`.

### Slide 2.4: `## Node Cutset: A Visual Example`
* **Before**: Highlight node $E$ in red on `gr_undir`.
* **After**: Remove node $E$ entirely (`filter(name != "E")`), showing that deleting $E$ also deletes all of its incident edges ($B-E, E-C, E-D$), completely isolating node $B$.
* **Layout**: Side-by-side (`p_before + p_after`), with `fig-width: 8` and `fig-height: 4.5`.
