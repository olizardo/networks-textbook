# Plan: Adding Directed Connectivity Slides with Larger 7-Node Graphs

This plan outlines the design and implementation of three dedicated slides in `slides/graph-connectivity.qmd` that systematically introduce and illustrate the three flavors of directed connectivity (Strong, Unilateral, and Weak) using larger, highly realistic 7-node directed graphs.

---

## 1. Visual Design of the 7-Node Digraphs

To ensure the graphs are clear and highly legible, we will use a symmetric circle layout for all three graphs, making it easy for students to trace directed edges:
* **Strongly Connected (`gr_large_strong`)**:
  - **The Graph**: A 7-node digraph containing a complete spanning cycle ($A \rightarrow B \rightarrow C \rightarrow D \rightarrow E \rightarrow F \rightarrow G \rightarrow A$) plus chords ($A \rightarrow D$, $C \rightarrow F$, $F \rightarrow B$).
  - **Why it is Strong**: A directed path exists between *every* pair of nodes, meaning all nodes are mutually reachable.
* **Unilaterally Connected (`gr_large_uni`)**:
  - **The Graph**: Same 7 nodes, but the feedback edge $G \rightarrow A$ and other backward chords are removed. Edges flow strictly forward ($A \rightarrow B \rightarrow C \rightarrow D \rightarrow E \rightarrow F \rightarrow G$ plus chords $A \rightarrow D$, $C \rightarrow F$, $B \rightarrow E$).
  - **Why it is Unilateral**: For any pair of nodes, there exists a directed path in at least one direction (e.g., $A$ can reach $G$, but $G$ cannot reach $A$).
* **Weakly Connected (`gr_large_weak`)**:
  - **The Graph**: Uses our textbook's 7-node digraph where $E$ and $G$ are transmitters (indegree 0).
  - **Why it is Weak**: There exist mutually unreachable pairs (e.g., $E$ cannot reach $G$ and $G$ cannot reach $E$), but they are connected when ignoring arrowheads (via semipaths).
