## 2. Structural Outline of the Combined Chapter

We will restructure `lesson-graphs-ties.qmd` using the following integrated outline:

### I. Introduction
*   Discuss the link between the sociological nature of relationships and mathematical graph structures.
*   Briefly state how we map social tie types to graphs to compute graph metrics.

### II. Symmetric Ties and Undirected Graphs
*   Define symmetric ties conceptually (directionless, built-in mutuality).
*   Define a **simple undirected graph** (no loops, no multiedges).
*   Include **`@fig-undirected`** (the 9-node undirected graph).
*   Provide real-world examples: similarities, co-location, co-memberships, and symmetrical roles (siblings).

### III. Asymmetric Ties and Directed Graphs
*   Define asymmetric ties conceptually (directionality, reciprocity is an empirical event).
*   Define a **simple directed graph** or digraph (no loops, single arc per direction).
*   Include **`@fig-directed`** (the directed graph).
*   Provide real-world examples: helping, advice, texting, social support.
*   In-depth discussion of **Reciprocity** (mutual vs. non-reciprocal, degree of reciprocity).
*   **Types of Nodes in Directed Graphs** (Receivers, Carriers, Transmitters; formulas for $k_{\text{in}}$ and $k_{\text{out}}$).

### IV. Visual and Sociological Taxonomies
*   **The 2x2 Graphical Classification Panel (`@fig-graph-theoretic-ties`)**: Integrate our newly perfected visual summary of Symmetric, Reciprocal, Non-Reciprocal, and Anti-Reciprocal ties.
*   **The Sociological Classification of Social Ties (`@tbl-sociological-ties`)**: Incorporate the States (Similarities, Social Relations) and Events (Interactions, Flows) matrix, followed by clean descriptions of each type.

### V. Special Classes of Directed Graphs
*   **Symmetric Directed Graphs** (`@fig-symm`): Where every connected pair is bi-directional.
*   **Functional Graphs** (`@fig-func`): Where the outdegree of every node is exactly one.
*   **Oriented Graphs** (`@fig-oriented`): Directed graphs containing no reciprocal dyads.
*   **Anti-Symmetric Ties and Tree Graphs** (`@fig-tree`): Uncomment and refine this section. Discuss how anti-symmetric relations (e.g., "is father of") concatenate to form a tree structure.

### VI. Real-World Structural Overlays
*   **The Anthropological Classification of Ties**: Detail organizational hierarchies (coworkers vs. bosses) and kinship networks (same-generation vs. cross-generation). Discuss **Transitivity** in these structures.
*   **Multiplexity**: Discuss how individuals are linked by multiple tie types simultaneously (e.g., supervisor and friend).
*   **The Growth of Social Networks**: Explain how networks evolve and merge (e.g., romantic and in-law ties joining separate family trees).
